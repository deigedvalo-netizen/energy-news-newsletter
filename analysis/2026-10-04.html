Crude Oil

Brent was reported back below $100 on Friday after reports of a stock release, but tankers were reported hit near Hormuz and prices stayed near that level. Crude flows through the strait have largely recovered, but CNBC notes it is unclear how long that can last because it relies on US military protection of tankers. Reserve releases are usually read by markets as a near-term supply offset, while renewed attacks on shipping are read as a risk to that recovery. Both are in play, and the net effect is unclear.

Natural Gas

LNG developers are the clearest source of corporate action. US export expansion is rising, with Venture Global and Cheniere both signing long-term sales agreements and Venture Global seeking FERC sign-off on Plaquemines Phase 1. Canada is a new entrant to the trend after the LNG Canada Phase 2 decision. Long-dated contracts of this kind are generally read as evidence of buyer appetite for secure supply, not as a statement on near-term prices. Record US production in July and five straight weeks of higher Atlantic LNG shipping rates are the other physical data points. South Korea's hesitance over Alaska LNG shows that not every announced project has committed buyers.

Refined Products

Diesel is the tightest part of the complex. UK diesel crossed £2 a litre, Russia extended its export ban, and the US has threatened to restrict exports unless Europe releases stocks.
